The Czech unreliable VAT payer list

Updated 22 October 2026 · about 4 minutes

The Czech tax administration publishes which VAT payers it has declared unreliable (nespolehlivý plátce). If you are a Czech VAT payer and buy from a supplier that is on this list, the law presumes you knew its VAT might not be paid, and you can be asked to pay that VAT yourself. See our guide to §109 VAT liability.

Who ends up on it

Under §106a of the VAT Act, the tax administrator decides that a VAT payer is unreliable if it has seriously breached its obligations in the administration of tax. The supplier can appeal within 15 days. There is also a separate status, unreliable person (nespolehlivá osoba, §106aa), for businesses that are not VAT payers. A payer can ask to be removed, but no earlier than one year after the decision became final (§106ac).

How to search it

  1. Open the VAT register (Registr plátců DPH), Financial Administration.
  2. Enter the supplier's DIČ without "CZ" (for a company this is usually its IČO). You can enter up to 20 numbers at once.
  3. Read the field Nespolehlivý plátce: NE means not unreliable, ANO means unreliable.
  4. Leave the number empty and choose "nespolehlivý plátce" to see the whole published list.

The register's VAT register help in English explains the search form.

Why one check is not enough

The status that matters is the one when the tax becomes due and when you pay, not when you signed the contract. A supplier that was fine in January can be published in March. That is why the Financial Administration and tax advisers recommend checking before each payment. If you already paid an unreliable payer, read what to do when a supplier becomes unreliable.
