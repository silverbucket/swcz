How to tell if a Czech company is in liquidation or has been struck off

Updated 22 October 2026 · about 4 minutes

A Czech company that is winding up does not disappear overnight. It goes through liquidation, which is recorded in the public registers and even in its name, and only afterwards is it deleted. This guide shows where each stage is visible and what it means if the company is your supplier.

The quick sign: "v likvidaci" after the name

Under §187(2) of the Civil Code, a legal entity enters liquidation on the day it is dissolved, and while in liquidation it uses its name with the addition "v likvidaci" (in liquidation). The liquidator must have the entry into liquidation recorded in the public register without undue delay.

The purpose of liquidation is to settle the company's assets, pay its creditors and deal with what is left (§187(1)). Once it is in liquidation, nobody may act for it beyond what the law allows for winding up (§188).

Where to check liquidation and deletion: ARES, the public register, ISIR

  1. ARES, Ministry of Finance: search the IČO. Look at the name, the status of the record, and the date of deletion (datum zániku) if any.
  2. Public register (Veřejný rejstřík), Ministry of Justice: open the company's extract (výpis). It shows entry into liquidation, the liquidator (likvidátor) and deletion from the register (výmaz).
  3. Insolvency register ISIR, Ministry of Justice: liquidation and insolvency are different procedures; check both.

What liquidation means for your orders and claims

  • Do not make advance payments to a company in liquidation without advice.
  • If it owes you money, file your claim with the liquidator.
  • Once a company is deleted from the register, it no longer exists.
