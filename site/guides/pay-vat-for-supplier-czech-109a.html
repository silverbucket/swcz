Paying VAT directly for your supplier (§109a)

Updated 22 October 2026 · about 4 minutes

Czech VAT law gives buyers a way to remove the risk of §109 liability: pay the supplier's VAT yourself, directly to the supplier's tax office. The law calls it zvláštní způsob zajištění daně, the special way of securing tax (§109a of the VAT Act).

What the law says

  • If the buyer pays the VAT on a supply for the supplier without having been called on as guarantor, the payment is used only for the supplier's VAT on that supply (§109a(1)).
  • The payment goes to the supplier's tax administrator (§109a(2)). It is credited to the supplier's personal deposit account and moved to its tax account when the VAT falls due (§109a(4)).
  • With the payment you must state, in the way the tax administrator publishes: the supplier's identification, the tax the payment is for, your own identification, and the date of the supply or of payment (§109a(2)). If the date is missing, the date the tax office receives the money is used (§109a(3)).

Why it helps

The Financial Administration's guidance on liability says a buyer who pays the VAT this way can never become guarantor under §109 for that supply. It is the clean option when a supplier turns out to be an unreliable VAT payer, or the invoice shows a bank account that is not published.

In practice

  1. Find the supplier's tax office (it is shown in the VAT register (Registr plátců DPH), Financial Administration).
  2. Look up that office's account and payment details on the Financial Administration website, as the law refers to the way the tax administrator publishes them.
  3. Pay the supplier the price without VAT, and the VAT to the tax office, with the required details.
  4. Tell the supplier, and keep proof of both payments.

Payments made this way are voluntary and the guidance says they are not refunded to you later, even if the supplier's VAT is corrected. Get your exact payment details confirmed by a tax adviser the first time.
