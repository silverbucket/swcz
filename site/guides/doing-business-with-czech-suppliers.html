Doing business with Czech suppliers: a guide for foreign companies

Updated 21 October 2026 · about 6 minutes

The Czech Republic has open, free public registers, so checking a new supplier is quick once you know the local terms. This guide covers what a foreign buyer should know.

Key terms

TermMeaning
IČOCompany identification number, 8 digits with a check digit. The main key for every register.
DIČTax / VAT identification number, "CZ" plus digits. For companies it is usually CZ + IČO.
s.r.o. / a.s.Limited liability company / joint-stock company.
Plátce DPHVAT payer (registered for Czech VAT).
Nespolehlivý plátceUnreliable VAT payer, published by the tax administration.
SídloRegistered office.

Step 1: confirm the company and its VAT number

  • Look the company up in ARES by IČO and compare the name and address with the contract and invoices.
  • For cross-border supplies within the EU, confirm the supplier's VAT number in the European Commission's VIES service. VIES shows whether a VAT number is valid for intra-EU trade.

Step 2: does the Czech §109 VAT liability apply to you?

The Czech rules that make a buyer liable for its supplier's unpaid VAT (§109 ZDPH) apply to buyers that are Czech VAT payers receiving taxable supplies with place of supply in the Czech Republic. That typically covers:

  • foreign companies registered for Czech VAT (for example because they hold stock, run projects or sell locally in the Czech Republic);
  • Czech subsidiaries and branches of foreign groups.

If your company is not registered for Czech VAT and buys goods that are shipped to another EU country, the supply is normally an intra-EU supply, and §109 is generally not the issue. You still have the commercial risks below. Your tax adviser can confirm which case you are in.

If §109 does apply: check the unreliable-payer status, and pay only to bank accounts that the supplier has published in the Czech VAT register. Since 1 January 2025 there is no minimum amount for the bank-account rule, and accounts abroad are not published in the Czech register.

Step 3: check financial health

  • Search the insolvency register ISIR by IČO before paying advances.
  • Look at filed annual accounts in the collection of documents (sbírka listin) at or.justice.cz. Many small companies file late, which is itself a signal.
  • For e-shops, check the ČOI risky e-shop list.

Step 4: keep watching

A supplier that is fine today can become an unreliable payer or file for insolvency next month. Re-check regularly, or let a monitoring service do it.
