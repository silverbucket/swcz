Your Czech supplier became an unreliable VAT payer: what to do

Updated 22 October 2026 · about 5 minutes

You found out that a supplier is now published as an unreliable VAT payer (nespolehlivý plátce). This guide covers the practical options. It applies if you are a Czech VAT payer buying a taxable supply in the Czech Republic.

1. Confirm the status and the date

Look the supplier up in the VAT register (Registr plátců DPH), Financial Administration and save a printout or screenshot. Note the date from which the supplier is published as unreliable. What matters for each invoice is whether the supplier was unreliable when the VAT on that supply became due or when you paid (§109(1) and (2)(a) of the VAT Act).

2. Open invoices: you can pay the VAT straight to the tax office

For an invoice you have not yet paid, you can pay the supplier only the price without VAT and pay the VAT part directly to the supplier's tax office. This is the special way of securing tax under §109a. The Financial Administration states that a buyer who pays the VAT this way cannot become guarantor for the VAT on that supply. You must do it before you are called on as guarantor. See how to pay VAT for a supplier under §109a.

Your contract may say you must pay the full amount to the supplier. Many Czech contracts include a clause allowing §109a payments; check yours, or agree it with the supplier.

3. Invoices you already paid

Liability is not automatic. The tax office first tries to collect the VAT from the supplier, and can only call on you as guarantor if the supplier does not pay. Keep your records of the checks you made. If a call (výzva ručiteli) arrives, talk to a tax adviser.

4. Decide about the relationship

An unreliable status means the tax office found a serious breach of tax duties. Consider asking for advance invoices to be dropped, shorter payment terms for them rather than you, or a different supplier. Check also the insolvency register.
