Checking a Czech supplier's published bank account

Updated 22 October 2026 · about 4 minutes

Czech VAT payers publish the bank accounts they use for business in the VAT register. When you pay a Czech VAT payer by bank transfer to an account that is not published, the law presumes you knew its VAT might go unpaid (§109(2)(c)(2) of the VAT Act). Since 1 January 2025, when §109 was rewritten by Act No. 461/2024 Sb., there is no minimum amount for this rule. Details are in our §109 guide.

Where the published accounts come from (§96 and §98)

A VAT payer lists all accounts used for its business when it registers and chooses which of them are published (§96). The tax administrator publishes the chosen accounts, or the fact that none was chosen (§98(d)).

How to check an invoice account against the VAT register

  1. Open the VAT register (Registr plátců DPH), Financial Administration and enter the supplier's DIČ without "CZ".
  2. Look at Bankovní účty (bank accounts) and the date each was published.
  3. Compare the account number on the invoice exactly, digit for digit, including the bank code.
  4. Use the account history (historie účtů) to see accounts whose publication ended.

What to do if the account on the invoice is not published

  • Do not pay to it until you have checked with the supplier through a contact you already trust, not the one on the invoice or email. See bank-account change fraud.
  • Ask the supplier to publish the account, or to give you a published one.
  • Or pay the VAT part directly to the tax office under §109a.

Accounts abroad are not published in the Czech register, so a transfer to a foreign account is treated the same way.
