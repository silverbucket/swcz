VIES vs the Czech VAT register

Updated 22 October 2026 · about 4 minutes

Companies outside the Czech Republic usually check a Czech supplier's VAT number in VIES, the European Commission's validation service. That is a useful check, but it answers a different question from the Czech register.

VIESCzech VAT register
Run byEuropean Commission, querying national databasesCzech Financial Administration
Tells youWhether the VAT number is valid for cross-border EU trade today, often with name and addressVAT payer status, unreliable payer status, published bank accounts, tax office, and the dates each changed
Main useSupporting zero-rated intra-EU suppliesAvoiding Czech §109 VAT liability, checking accounts

What VIES tells you about a Czech VAT number, and what it does not

VIES passes your query to the Czech database and returns valid or invalid for the moment you ask. It keeps no history, so it cannot tell you whether a number was valid on the date you paid an invoice last month. Save the consultation number VIES offers; that is your evidence of the check. VIES says nothing about unreliable status, bank accounts or insolvency.

When you need the Czech VAT register as well

  • You are registered for Czech VAT and buy supplies taxed in the Czech Republic: §109 applies to you.
  • You pay a Czech supplier and want to confirm the account is theirs: see published bank accounts.
  • You want to know when a supplier became a VAT payer or stopped being one: the Czech register shows the dates.

Frequently asked questions

Does VIES show Czech unreliable payer status?

No. Only the Czech VAT register does. See the unreliable payer list.

My supplier is valid in VIES but has no published bank account. Is that a problem?

For a buyer that is a Czech VAT payer, yes: paying to an unpublished account triggers the §109 presumption. For a foreign buyer with no Czech VAT registration it is a fraud warning sign rather than a tax risk.

Which should I check first?

For a one-off cross-border purchase, VIES. For a Czech supplier you will pay repeatedly, the Czech register, and VIES as well if you zero-rate your own supplies to them.
